Description:

People move in unique ways that can be indicative of their age, gender, and even neurologic health. In this project, our goal is to algorithmically learn elements of the underlaying structure in natural, unrestricted, motion in people across demographics. We call these structural elements motion strategies. We hope these motion strategies will bring to light differences between people in different demographic groups, new subgroups of people in similar demographics, and which motion strategies may be associated with different developmental movement disorders. Eventually, we hope to use our understanding of inherent motion strategies to develop methods of generating more realistic and diverse digital character controls.

Datasets:

  • Custom-made augmented reality game for collection of semi-constrained upper-limb reaching mocap data. Deployed at the Minnesota State Fair in 2023 and 2024. Collected movement data from over 550 typically-developing, healthy, people aged 6+. See example screenshot below.
  • In collaboration with the Department of Neonatology and a number of different children's hospitals accross the twin cities, natural movement data of over 400 pre-term infants at risk of cerebral palsy.

Methods currently being explored:

  • Spline fitting
  • Tensor Component Analysis (TCA)
  • Variational autoencoder (VAE) with latent space clustering
  • Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) with latent space clustering
  • Graph Convolutional Network (GCN)
  • Attention-based Transformer Model

Challenges faced:

  • Data cleaning through sections of dropped tracking frames - if the dropped section is small enough, fill with last known point
  • Identifying the best network structure take advantage of the data dimensionality - want to maintain a dimension for participants, frames, track-points, and physical dimension
